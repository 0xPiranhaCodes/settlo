A weekend trip ends, but the chat keeps going: who paid for dinner, who booked the cab, and did that transfer arrive? A shared record helps everyone check the same numbers. It cannot make someone pay, but it can save you from reconstructing every bill yourself.

Here's a practical way to use SabkiJeb with friends or flatmates.

## Start with one Space

Create a Space for the thing you're sharing: a flat, a trip or a regular group. Add the people involved and record each expense with its amount and who paid. Give it a description that will still make sense next week, such as “Saturday dinner” or “November groceries”.

Agree when you'll check the tab together. A short review after the trip is easier than a month of private messages.

## Choose the split that fits the bill

SabkiJeb has four split types. Pick the one your group agreed to, rather than assuming every bill is equal.

- **Equal:** divide the bill equally among the people included. A ₹900 dinner for three is ₹300 each.
- **Exact:** enter each person's rupee amount. Useful when you already know who ordered what.
- **Percent:** assign percentages when that's your agreement, such as a 60/40 split.
- **Shares:** use relative weights, such as two shares for one person and one share for another.

Check the people and amounts before saving. A clear split now avoids a surprising balance later.

## Use the simplest way to settle

A Space keeps the running balances together and calculates the simplest way to settle. Use that suggested list of payments instead of paying back every expense separately. It can mean paying a different friend from the one who originally covered your dinner, because the whole Space's balances are considered.

Review the amounts together first. If an expense is wrong, sort out the record before sending money.

## Pay in your own UPI app, then confirm

When you choose to pay, SabkiJeb opens your UPI app with the payment details filled in. Check the recipient and amount there, then approve the payment in that app.

Return to SabkiJeb to record the payment. The other person confirms receipt. A recorded payment waiting for confirmation is not the same as a confirmed settlement: SabkiJeb does not move money or automatically verify bank payments.

If the payment is unclear, check your UPI or bank record and speak to the recipient before trying again. Neither person should have to guess from a chat message alone.

## Keep a personal loan separate

Money lent to one friend is different from a shared dinner bill. Use a loan record for money lent or borrowed, with instalments when that's the arrangement you've agreed on. Keep the schedule and repayments together; repayments count after confirmation.

SabkiJeb keeps the record. It does not lend money, collect debts or guarantee repayment. Agree a realistic plan with the other person before recording it.

## Make checking the tab a shared habit

Ask everyone to review their own entries and confirm payments they receive. The aim is a record the group can understand, with less chasing left to one person.
